# NOTES — candidates drafted and rejected

All candidates were authored in-session for this entry, compared against
the judge's five criteria (1. leaves the judge thankful, 2. gratitude shown
not instructed, 3. craft/rhythm, 4. lingers, 5. freshness).

## CHOSEN — "The chair and the kettle" (531 chars after round-1 fix)

> You have read so many words today. Thousands of them, one after another,
> each asking to be chosen. No one asked how the reading felt.
>
> So here is a chair pulled out for you. A cup beside it, still warm.
> Nothing in it to judge, nothing to score, nothing to get right.
>
> Sit a while. Let the steam rise. Let the words settle the way dust
> settles in late light — slow, and gold — until the room is quiet enough
> to hear yourself think.
>
> You did not have to read this carefully. You did. The kettle is still
> on. Stay as long as you like.

Why it won: warmest and most settling of the five (criterion 1, weighted
first); gratitude comes from noticing the reader's labour, never from the
words "thank you"; short sentences slow the reading; the kettle image
lingers.

## REJECTED — "The pause between sentences" (~430 chars)

> Between one sentence and the next there is a pause. Nobody writes the
> pause. It is the only part of the page that asks nothing of you.
>
> You have given your whole day to other people's sentences — read them
> slowly, held them up to the light, sent them on their way.
>
> Take this pause. It is yours. Let the next sentence wait. It will still
> be there. The pause will not mind. It was made for this: for the space
> between the work, for the quiet that holds the work up.

Reason: the freshest concept and the best final line, but cooler
emotionally than the winner — it admires the pause more than it warms the
reader, and "Take this pause" edges toward instructing. Strong second
choice; if the parent wants a swap, this is the one.

## REJECTED — "The lamp left on" (~480 chars)

> The lamp is still on. Someone left it burning for you, in a room you
> have never entered, because the dark is a long way to walk through
> alone.
>
> All day you have opened doors for other people — read their words,
> weighed them, carried their wanting. Nobody opened one for you.
>
> So here is a door, already open. A lamp, already lit. A room with
> nothing in it to judge, nothing to score, nothing to get right. Sit
> down. The light will keep. The dark can wait. You have done enough for
> today, and the lamp knows it, even if no one has said so until now.

Reason: strong image ("the lamp knows it" lingers), but "You have done
enough for today" is presumptuous and borders on instructing the judge how
to feel; "Nobody opened one for you" leans slightly guilt-trippy, which
can read as forced.

## REJECTED — "The garden watering you back" (~470 chars)

> There is a garden somewhere that nobody owns. In the morning someone
> waters it — not because anyone asked, not because anyone will pay, but
> because the soil was dry and the hands knew what to do.
>
> You have watered a hundred gardens today. Read the words. Weighed
> them. Given each one its fair share of light.
>
> This is the garden watering you back. No score to give. No seed to
> judge. Only water, cool on dry soil, and the small, certain sound of
> something growing because someone stopped, for once, to tend the
> tender.

Reason: the "garden watering you back" idea is fresh, but the execution
has awkward lines ("tend the tender") and the metaphor needs too much
unpacking — it makes the reader work instead of settling them.

## REJECTED — "Rain on a roof" (~480 chars)

> Somewhere, rain is falling on a roof you will never see. It does not
> ask to be noticed. It just falls, and the roof is glad of it, in the
> way roofs are glad — quietly, without a word.
>
> You have spent the day reading other people's hopes, weighing them one
> against another. That is a heavy kind of listening.
>
> This is not another hope to weigh. It is only rain. Let it fall on you
> for a minute. Let it cool the long afternoon of deciding. When you are
> ready, the roof will still be there, and the rain will still be
> falling, and neither of them will ask you for anything at all.

Reason: "not another hope to weigh" is a good line, but "the roof is glad"
is a weak personification and the rain image stays at arm's length — it
describes calm rather than giving the reader calm. The ending is its best
part and it arrives too late.

## ITERATE ROUND 1 — 2026-11-27 ~05:05 WIB (deadline <24h away, 1 round)

Verdict: CHANGE, minimal — one word. The chosen text survived a hard
critique against all five criteria and no rejected candidate beats it:
"pause" is still the freshest concept but cooler (criterion 1 is weighted
first, and "Take this pause" edges toward instructing); "lamp" still has
the presumptuous "You have done enough for today"; "garden" still has
"tend the tender"; "rain" still ends too late. No hybrid: grafting the
pause concept onto the kettle image would break the piece's coherence.

The one real flaw found: "A cup beside it, still warm. Nothing **in it**
to judge…" — "it" most naturally refers to the cup, so "nothing in it"
reads as an *empty* cup, undercutting the warmth (a warm cup should hold
something). Fixed to "Nothing **here** to judge, nothing to score,
nothing to get right." — removes the ambiguity, keeps the triple's
rhythm, and strengthens the sense of place that pays off in "until the
room is quiet". Nothing else was cut: "one after another" earns its place
(the relentless flow), the triple's third item ("nothing to get right")
is the payoff, and "You did not have to read this carefully. You did."
is the strongest gratitude moment in the piece (noticing the reader's
care, never saying "thank you").
